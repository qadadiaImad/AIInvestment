Assalamu alaykum. I'm Karim. You want to grow your money... without trading away what you believe. Let me walk you through halal investing, in plain words.
Halal finance is a filter. It avoids companies that make their money from interest, from gambling, from things Muslims stay away from. And it avoids debt-heavy bets that look more like a casino than a business.
So how do you check a stock? You run a screen. Three questions.
One: what does the company sell? If more than about five percent of its income comes from impure sources, it gets flagged.
Two: how much does it borrow? Of every hundred dollars of company value, borrowed money should stay under about thirty.
Three: where does its cash sit? Money parked in interest-bearing accounts gets capped the same way.
Pass all three... and the stock clears the screen.
One more idea: purification. Even a clean company might earn a small impure slice. Say, bank interest. That slice doesn't belong in your pocket. You calculate it, per share... and give it to charity.
That's the whole system. Screen. Own. Purify.
And remember. A screen is a computed result, not a fatwa. For your own situation, ask a scholar. Educational, not financial or religious advice.
