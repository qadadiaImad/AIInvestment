## 2. Full Plain-Text Script

There is no one right way to trade. Each style ahead differs by time required, temperament, and goals — and the right fit depends on you.

Scalping captures tiny price moves within seconds to minutes, repeated many times each session. It demands constant screen time, low-latency execution, and strict risk control, since trading costs add up fast.

Day trading opens and closes positions within one session, avoiding overnight exposure entirely. It still demands hours of focused attention, and in the US, meeting the pattern day trader rule's equity requirement.

Swing trading holds positions for several days to a few weeks, aiming to capture one directional leg within a larger trend. It requires patience to sit through pullbacks without exiting early.

Position trading holds a trade for weeks or months, following a broader trend rather than daily noise. It ties up capital for long stretches and requires conviction through normal volatility.

Trend trading stays with a market as long as it makes higher highs and higher lows. The trade can run weeks or months, and the key skill is holding through pullbacks.

Breakout trading waits for price to compress into a tight range, then enters once that range breaks. Because many breakouts fail, the style depends on fast decisions and a predefined stop-loss.

Range trading buys near support and sells near resistance, betting the price stays boxed between two levels. It requires close monitoring, since a breakout ends the range and can trigger losses fast.

News trading positions a trade around a scheduled release, aiming to catch the sudden price reaction it triggers. The window is short, spreads widen fast, and execution speed matters more than analysis.

An algorithmic system trades on pre-defined code rather than discretion, executing entries and exits the instant its conditions are met. Building and maintaining one demands programming skill and constant technical oversight.

Portfolio and sizing decisions determine how capital is spread across positions and adjusted as market conditions change. This work unfolds over weeks or months, rewarding patience rather than quick reaction.

The best style is not the cleverest one, it is the one you can sustain, trade after trade. This is educational content, not financial advice.

## 3. Delivery Notes

**Overall pace:** Calm and measured, roughly 150–160 words per minute — noticeably slower than conversational speech. Never rush the last few words of a segment to "make it fit"; a slight undershoot before the next segment starts is preferable to compressed delivery.

**Overall tone:** Steady, informed, even-keeled — the voice of a knowledgeable narrator explaining mechanics, not a hype presenter selling an outcome. Keep energy consistent across all ten styles; do not brighten for the "exciting" ones (scalping, news trading) or flatten for the "slow" ones (position, portfolio). Consistency of tone reinforces the script's core claim that no style is superior.

**Pauses:**
- A full breath-length pause (roughly 0.5–0.7s) after the intro's final word, before style 1 begins.
- A short pause (roughly 0.3–0.4s) between the two sentences within every style segment — the first sentence defines the style, the second states its demands. Let the demand sentence land as a distinct, weighed statement, not a continuation.
- A slightly longer pause after "It requires patience..." (style 3) and "the key skill is holding through pullbacks" (style 5) — these are the two segments about endurance, and the pause lets the idea settle.
- A clear pause before the close segment, and a distinct pause between the close's main sentence and the compliance line, so the disclaimer reads as a separate, deliberate statement rather than a trailing afterthought.

**Emphasis (words to carry slightly more weight, not volume):**
- Intro: *no one right way*, *depends on you*
- Scalping: *seconds to minutes*, *fast*
- Day Trading: *within one session*, *equity requirement*
- Swing Trading: *several days to a few weeks*, *without exiting early*
- Position Trading: *weeks or months*, *conviction*
- Trend Trading: *higher highs and higher lows*, *holding through pullbacks*
- Breakout Trading: *compress*, *fast decisions*
- Range Trading: *boxed between two levels*, *fast*
- News Trading: *scheduled release*, *speed*
- Algorithmic: *pre-defined code*, *constant technical oversight*
- Portfolio / Sizing: *spread across positions*, *patience*
- Close: *sustain*, *educational content, not financial advice*

**What to avoid:** No exclamation-mark energy, no rising inflection at segment ends (each style should land as a flat, settled statement), no editorializing pauses that imply ranking between styles.
