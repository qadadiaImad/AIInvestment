## Fundamental walkthrough (third person)

Meta closed July 3 at $582.90 while analysts' fundamental-value model estimates the business is worth $813.62 per share — a 28.4% discount. The single biggest factor behind that estimate is growth priced like maturity: revenue grew 26.18% year over year, roughly four times the Internet Software/Services industry median of 6.79%, yet the stock trades at just 21.19x trailing earnings against an industry median of 15.82x. The quality of that growth is unusual — an 81.94% gross margin versus a 57.71% industry median, and a 32.84% net margin in an industry whose median company barely breaks even at 0.54%. Returns on capital confirm it: 32.93% return on equity (industry median 0.27%) with a lightly levered balance sheet (debt-to-equity 0.36, current ratio 2.35). The 6.95x price-to-sales multiple looks rich next to the 1.93x industry median, but that premium is what an 82-cent gross-margin dollar commands over a 58-cent one. The tension is real: the shares fell 18.51% over the past year while the business grew 26%, which is precisely how the model's gap widened — the market is pricing the AI capital-spending bill and a reorg that reportedly "hasn't come to fruition," not the current income statement. Whether the $813.62 or the $582.90 is right depends on whether that spending becomes a second business — which is exactly what the reported "Meta Compute" launch claims to be.
