## Primary — Instagram

> A line at 437 is worth nothing because somebody drew it.
>
> It's worth something because the market kept coming back to it.
>
> Zoom into the setup and you see a cup and handle — nice shape, nothing more. Pull the camera back two years and the same level shows up ten separate times. Sellers defended it in 2021. Buyers defended it in 2023. That's not a line on a chart, that's a price people keep making decisions at.
>
> So when the S&P 500 closed back above it, the trade wasn't "the pattern looks good." The trade was: price reclaimed a level it had been fighting over for two years.
>
> Entry at the close that took it back. Stop under the handle — because if it falls back into the cup, the setup never happened, and that's worth 8.08. Target 2x the risk, chosen before anyone knew the outcome.
>
> Twelve sessions later it got there.
>
> The part that actually matters isn't that this one worked. It's that the exit was decided before the entry was taken. Not every one of these reaches its target — which is exactly why you set the stop first.
