# Market Lessons with Sol — ep.2 "Sixteen Minutes"

Target ~2:00 (3600f @30fps). Same cast, same set, same rails.

## The thesis

Episode 1: *public information is not enough — you can't copy what you
can't see.*

Episode 2 is the other half: **somebody saw it.** The tape moved before
the public did, twice, in nine figures — and the investigation is still
looking for who.

## THE ACCURACY RAIL — read before touching a line

Everything below is drawn from mainstream reporting and official
congressional letters. Three hard rules:

1. **No named official is depicted as a trader or a suspect.** I checked
   specifically: no reporting connects the Treasury Secretary or the
   Defense Secretary to these trades. They appear in this story only as
   people who made public statements. Drawing them as manipulators would
   be inventing an accusation against living, identifiable officials.
2. **The traders are UNKNOWN and the episode says so, repeatedly.** No
   charges have been filed in the oil case. "Somebody" is the subject of
   every sentence about the trades — never a name, never an implication.
3. **The one charged case is named accurately and kept separate.** The
   Polymarket/Maduro indictment is a real, unsealed federal case about a
   *different* event. It is the proof the mechanism works, not evidence
   about the oil trades.

Footer on every frame, as in ep.1:
`parody · public record · educational, not advice · no accusation`

## Beat sheet

### ACT 1 — the clock (~25s)
1. **SOL** Kid. Forget the filings for a minute. I want to show you a clock.
2. **REX** A clock.
3. **SOL** March twenty-third. Six forty-nine in the morning. Somebody buys five hundred and eighty million dollars of oil futures.
4. **REX** Okay. Big trade. Somebody's bullish.
5. **SOL** No. They were betting oil would FALL. And stocks would rise.
   → **GRAPHIC:** the 16-minute countdown timeline begins

### ACT 2 — the sixteen minutes (~30s)
6. **SOL** Sixteen minutes later, the President posts that talks with Iran went well.
7. **SOL** Oil falls. Stocks rise. Exactly the way that trade was pointed.
   → **GRAPHIC:** timeline completes — trade at 6:49, post at 7:05, price drop
8. **REX** ...that's a coincidence, right? Tell me that's a coincidence.
9. **SOL** Once is a coincidence, kid.

### ACT 3 — it happened again (~35s)
10. **SOL** Two weeks later. Nine hundred and fifty million, betting oil falls. Hours before a ceasefire nobody had announced yet.
    → **GRAPHIC:** second spike, stacked on the first
11. **SOL** And a separate one — seven hundred and sixty million, minutes before the Hormuz announcement.
12. **REX** How much is that all together?
13. **SOL** The Justice Department and the CFTC are looking at about two point six billion.
    → **GRAPHIC:** the running total
14. **REX** So they caught them.
15. **SOL** No.

### ACT 4 — nobody knows who (~35s)
16. **SOL** That's the part I need you to sit with. Not one charge. Not one name. The tape knew, and the tape doesn't sign its orders.
    → **CARICATURE:** the podium — officials announcing
    → **CUT TO:** an anonymous silhouette with the ticket
17. **REX** Somebody has to know something.
18. **SOL** Four members of Congress have written asking exactly that.
19. **REX** And?
20. **SOL** And the investigation is open. That's where it sits.

### ACT 5 — but they CAN catch you (~30s)
21. **SOL** Now — they did catch one. Different war, same idea.
22. **SOL** A Special Forces soldier bet a prediction market on an operation he'd been briefed on. Classified. Indicted.
    → **GRAPHIC:** the charged case, separated visually from the oil probe
23. **REX** So it IS provable.
24. **SOL** When the trail leads somewhere, yes.
25. **REX** And when it doesn't?
26. **SOL** Then all you have is the clock.

### ACT 6 — the lesson (~15s)
27. **SOL** Last week I told you public information isn't enough to find an edge.
28. **SOL** This week: somebody had an edge sixteen minutes before the public had a headline.
29. **REX** So what do I do with THAT?
30. **SOL** You stop believing the news moves the market, kid. Sometimes the market moves first — and the news catches up.
31. **SOL** Fair? No. But now you know what to watch.
